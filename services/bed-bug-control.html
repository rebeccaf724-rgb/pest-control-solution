Biological Cycle & Treatment

Why Bed Bugs Require a Two-Round Treatment

Bed bugs (known as Mankunalu in Telugu) are nocturnal blood-suckers that shelter in microscopic gaps inside mattress piping, cot joints, electrical switch plates, and behind headboards. A female bed bug lays between 200 and 500 eggs in her lifetime.

The Egg Shell Dilemma: No chemical in the world safely penetrates hard bed bug egg casings without damaging household furniture. That is why single-visit treatments almost always fail after 10 to 14 days when the eggs hatch into voracious nymphs.

Our Proven 2-Round Eradication Protocol

  1. Intensive Round 1 (Day 1): High-knockdown targeted residual spray applied to mattresses, box springs, bed frames, skirting boards, wardrobes, sofa seams, and wall switchboards. Knocks down all living adult bed bugs and walking nymphs within hours.
  2. Egg Incubation Window (12–15 Days): Residual formulation remains active while remaining unhatched eggs slowly incubate and hatch into first-stage nymphs.
  3. Mandatory Round 2 (Day 15): A second thorough spray to destroy every newly emerged nymph before they reach sexual maturity or lay new eggs, completely collapsing the reproductive cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Bed Bug Treatment FAQs

Clear answers about preparing your home and eliminating bed bugs permanently.

Do we need to throw away our mattress?

No! In over 95% of cases, discarding mattresses is completely unnecessary. Our contact and residual spray thoroughly sanitizes seams, beading, and cushions without damaging the fabric or memory foam.

How should we prepare our bedrooms before the technician arrives?

Remove bedsheets, pillowcases, and blankets and wash them in hot water. Clear items stored underneath the beds and leave closets unlocked so our technicians can inspect perimeter corners.

How long should we stay outside after spraying?

We recommend keeping the treated rooms closed for 2 to 3 hours following the application. Afterwards, open doors and windows for 20 minutes to ventilate. You can sleep in your bedroom the same night.

Is the second visit really necessary?

Yes, absolutely. Round 2 (done 12 to 15 days later) is essential to eliminate baby nymphs that were protected inside eggs during the first visit. This prevents them from growing up and laying a new generation.
